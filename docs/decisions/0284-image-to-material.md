# 0284. Image to material: an Image box and four plain steps from a photo to a seamless material

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-13

## Problem

Phase 12's sixth extra (milestone 21, [decision 0269](0269-material-extras.md)): **bring in a photo or a painting** and turn it into a material. The app should make it **tile seamlessly**, **remove the light and shadow baked into it**, and **guess its height and roughness**, in plain, explained steps with "What does this do?" words, using the image reader the app already has (PNG, JPEG, TGA, BMP), with the picture **stored on the box, packed**, scaled to the graph's size. It must be possible to **choose the square part** of a photo that isn't square and to set its **real-world size**. Refusals explain (a file that can't be read, one too big). No AI models, no new heavy dependencies, permissive licences only, and no new keys (files are picked through the existing open dialog). Milestone 20 had warned that graph files over 16 MB can't be read, so big pictures must be stored efficiently.

## Options

- **One box with every step as settings, or a chain of boxes.** One Image box with sections (crop, tiling, light, height, roughness) would be compact, but its many settings would hide what each step does, and there would be no picture of each step. **A small chain of boxes** shows a live picture after every step (the graph's own way of explaining itself), lets each step be bypassed to compare (decision 0278), and each step is useful on its own (Make seamless on any picture, Even out light on a painted colour). To keep it easy, the Image box's **Make into material** button adds the whole chain, wired and framed, in one undo step.
- **Making it tile.** *Mirroring* the picture (seamless, but obviously symmetrical); *patch-based synthesis* (quilting, texture synthesis: the best looks, but slow, many settings, and it can fail in ways that are hard to explain); **offset and blend**: a copy moved half a texture along has its joins in the middle, so near the edges show that copy and in the middle the picture, blended between. It is what artists do by hand, always works, and is explained in one sentence. Its weakness, washed-out ghosting where the two copies blend, is fixed by a **contrast-keeping blend** (each value's difference from the average divided by how much the mix shrinks it) and by **wobbling** the line where they meet with noise, so it never runs straight.
- **Taking out baked light.** Fitting a smooth surface (a plane or a curve) only catches light that changes steadily; **a high-pass against a heavy blur** (divide each pixel by the blurred lightness, times the average) catches any large-scale light and shadow, keeps the detail, and is one idea: "it blurs the picture until only the big patches of light are left, then evens those out". A photo's far edges have nothing to do with each other, so before it tiles the blur mustn't wrap round: past an edge the picture is **carried on as it was heading** (the edge's value twice less the one as far inside), which keeps a steady change of light steady right to the edge.
- **Guessing height and roughness.** Machine-learnt guesses were ruled out (no models). Height from **lightness**, keeping only changes smaller than a **Detail size** (lightness less a blur of it, so a shadow across the photo isn't a slope), stretched so the darkest and lightest few hundredths reach black and white; roughness from **how busy each spot is** (the spread of lightness round it), laid between a **Shiniest** and a **Roughest**. Both are honest guesses and say so.
- **Storing the picture.** As a `Painting` (square only, RGBA tiles packed with zstd: a photo packs badly, and a list of numbers in JSON graph files took about ten bytes a byte); as PNG (lossless, but a 2048-pixel photo is 6 to 9 MB); **as a JPEG**, packed once when brought in at quality 92 (lower, down to 60, only when a picture would take more than 3 MB), with the pixels then taken from that JPEG so the photo is exactly the same after saving and opening, and saving never packs it again. A photo is kept at most **2048 pixels** on its longer side. In JSON (graph and box files) packed bytes are now written as **base64 text** (a third larger than the bytes, not ten times); lists of numbers from format 3 files are still read.

## Decision

- **Image** (`image`, Make; `matgraph::boxes::image_box`): no inputs; outputs **Colour** and **Lightness** (a height). It holds a `matgraph::Photo` (`photo.rs`: sRGB bytes, three a pixel, any shape up to `MAX_PHOTO_SIDE` 2048, its file's name, the packed bytes it is saved as, a key hashed from its pixels), on `GraphBox::photo`, kept only by kinds whose `BoxType::takes_photo`, changed by `GraphEdit::SetPhoto` (one undo step, "Bring in a picture"), given to the job (`Job::photo`), hashed into its key, copied by `copy_edits`. Settings **Square size** (`part`, 0.05 to 1 of the shorter side), **Left to right** (`across`) and **Top to bottom** (`down`) choose the square part; `Photo::sampled` scales it to the graph's size, averaging up to 8 × 8 samples a pixel so a big photo made small doesn't shimmer. With no picture, the box refuses in plain words ("No picture brought in yet. Choose Bring in a picture… in Attributes…").
- **Even out light** (`even_light`, Colour): Colour in and out; **Strength** (1), **Light patch size** (0.25 of the texture), **Edges** (Tiling, the standard, wrapping round; or Photo, for a picture not made seamless yet).
- **Make seamless** (`seamless`, Shape): a height, a colour or a material in, each made seamless (normals blended and made a unit long); **Blend width** (0.25 of the texture from each edge), **Wobble** (0.6), **Seed**. Two passes, across then down, each blending the picture with its half-texture-moved copy by a mask that is exactly 1 at the edges and 0 from the blend width in.
- **Guess height** (`guess_height`, Shape): Colour in, Height out; **Detail size** (0.1), **Big shapes** (0.25: some plain lightness added back, so whole stones stand up), **Smoothing** (0.3), **Invert**.
- **Guess roughness** (`guess_roughness`, Shape): Colour in, **Roughness** (greys) out; **Shiniest** (0.45), **Roughest** (0.9), **Detail size** (0.02), **Invert**.
- **Material from height** gains a **Roughness** input (greys; its Roughness setting is used when nothing is plugged in), added after its released plugs.
- **Attributes for an Image box** (`ui::material::from_photo`): what it holds ("wall.jpg, kept at 2048 × 1536 pixels"), **Bring in a picture…** / **Choose another picture…** (the app's open dialog through `WorkbenchAction::ChoosePhoto`; a double-click on the box does the same), **the seam check** of its picture in plain words (also on Make seamless), **Real-world size** (Material out's own setting, edited here as well, with a hint to measure a brick or a plank in the photo; one value for the graph, in one place), and **Make into material**: Even out light (Edges: Photo) → Make seamless → Guess height and Guess roughness → Material from height (colour, height, roughness) → Material out (added if missing; wired if free; otherwise left beside it, with a note), tidied, inside a comment frame **From a photo** whose note lists the steps, one undo step.
- **Reading pictures** (`fileio::image_file::read_photo`, `open_photo`, `photo_from_packed`): through the existing image reader (`read_image`, sizes limited before pixels are read, fuzz-tested); see-through parts laid over mid grey; made smaller to fit 2048 (shape kept); packed as described. Refusals are the reader's plain words ("it isn't an image the app reads (PNG, JPEG, TGA or BMP)", "it is damaged", "it is larger than the app reads"), shown by the app's import note with the file's name.
- **Saved:** project file format **108** (`fileio::project::v108`, `v95::BoxV95::photo`: the name and the packed JPEG, unpacked through the image reader and refused when bigger than an Image box keeps). Graph and box files read format 4 and write it only when a box holds a photo. Packed bytes are base64 text in JSON (decision above). The undo history counts a photo only when a change's before and after don't share it (`scene::memory`).
- **No new keys or mouse actions**: buttons, the existing open dialog, and the double-click that already opens a box.

## Consequences

A photo becomes a seamless material in two clicks, and every step can be looked through, bypassed or tuned. The guesses are guesses: a photo with strong shadows between stones, or dark parts that stick out, needs Invert or a hand from Levels; the manual says how to take a good photo for it. Offset and blend can show two rows of a strong regular pattern (bricks, planks) where the copies meet; a narrower Blend width or choosing the square part so the rows meet up helps, and a patch-based method could be added later as a second choice. The seam check can call a picture with a straight line exactly along its edge "joined" (a false alarm also seen with Tiles' Random); it says what it sees. Milestone 22 (stamp images) can keep its pictures as `Photo`s on the box the same way.
