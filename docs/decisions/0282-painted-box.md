# 0282. The Painted box: painting inside a material graph with the painter's brushes

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-13

## Problem

Phase 12's fifth extra (milestone 20, [decision 0269](0269-material-extras.md)): a **Painted** box you brush on in the Material editor, a mask, a colour or a height, for things placed by hand (a crack just there, moss in one corner), **using the painter's brushes**: the same brush engine, shelf, brush settings and controls the project lead chose for painting ([decision 0177](0177-texture-painter-plan.md) and after), with no new keys. The brief asked for: choosing the kind on the box; "Paint on it" from the box's right-click menu and Attributes opening a flat painting view of its texture, tiled 3 × 3 with strokes wrapping over the edges so it stays seamless, with an optional faint underlay of the box's input or the graph's result; Tab back to the graph, Esc cancelling a stroke; the paint kept at a chosen resolution with the project (compressed when large), scaled to the graph's size; undo per stroke, erase, clear; an input to paint over or blend with; and painting on the model in the preview only if cheap.

## Options

- **What the kind changes.** Plugs that change with a setting (one output that is a mask, a colour or a height) were considered and left in [decision 0270](0270-tiling-and-moving-colours.md): every place reading a plug's kind would need the box's settings. Three kinds of box (Painted mask, Painted colour, Painted height) with the Kind switch swapping the box would keep one plug each, but swapping a box means rewiring, and variations, dots and frames refer to it by id. One box with **an input and an output of each kind**, of which **only the chosen kind's output gives anything** (the others say why when wired), keeps plugs fixed and is honest about what it gives.
- **Where the paint lives.** A paint layer (`paint::Image`) can't sit in `matgraph`, which `paint` depends on. A painting type in `matgraph` with **the painter's tile shape** (64 pixels square, RGBA bytes, each tile shared) converts to and from `paint::Image` without copying, so strokes, undo and saving all share tiles.
- **When a stroke reaches the graph.** Every frame (the boxes after it update as you paint, but the graph is worked out again and the painting hashed again many times a second), or **when the stroke ends** (the view shows the stroke as it goes from the stroke's own image; one change to the graph a stroke). Ending the stroke keeps the editor smooth on big paintings and gives one undo step a stroke for free.
- **Wrapping.** Painting each dab nine times (once per copy) or **one dab whose texels wrap round**, taking each texel's nearest copy of the dab when the dab is wider than the texture.
- **How it is saved.** Tile by tile, as paint layers are (fine in the project file, which is packed as a whole), or **the pixels packed with zstd** (graph and box files are JSON, where tiles of raw bytes would be huge).

## Decision

- **The box:** **Painted** (`painted`, Detail group; `matgraph::boxes::painted`). Inputs **Mask**, **Height**, **Colour** (what it paints over); outputs **Mask** (`mask_out`), **Height** (`height_out`), **Colour** (`colour_out`). Settings: **Kind** (`kind`: Mask, Height, Colour; Mask first), **Blend** (`blend`: Over, Add, Subtract, Multiply), **Strength** (`strength`, 0 to 1). Only the chosen kind's output gives anything (`BoxType::run_outputs`); a box wired to another says "This Painted box paints a mask, so its Colour output gives nothing. Change its Kind to Colour in Attributes, or connect its Mask output instead." (a new `BoxType::why_empty`, used by the evaluator's empty-output refusal). Where nothing is painted it gives its input of that kind, or black (a mask), mid grey (a height) or grey (a colour). For a mask or a height the paint counts by its **lightness**, as the painter's masks read paint.
- **The painting:** `matgraph::Painting` (`painting.rs`): square, one of **256, 512, 1024 (new ones) or 2048** pixels, RGBA in the painter's 64-pixel tiles, empty tiles left out, with a **key** hashed from its pixels (so the same painting opened from a file has the same key and the box's result is reused). `GraphBox::painting`, kept only by a kind whose `BoxType::takes_painting` (others drop it when read), changed only by `GraphEdit::SetPainting` (checked again as a file's would be; `None` or an empty painting clears it), given to the job (`Job::painting`) and hashed into its key, copied by `copy_edits` (pastes, your own boxes). `Painting::sampled` scales it to the graph's size, averaging blocks when smaller and blending between pixels, wrapping round, when larger. `paint::Image::from_painting` / `to_painting` share tiles.
- **The painting view** (`ui::material::paint_canvas`): **Paint on it** in the box's right-click menu and in Attributes, and a double-click on the box (as a Shape box opens its canvas), put it in the node canvas's place. Its bar: **Back to the graph (Tab)**, the **Kind** (Mask, Height, Colour), the **resolution** (changing it scales the paint, one undo step; with nothing painted yet it sets the size the first stroke starts at), **what shows under** (Nothing, Its input (standard), The result) with a **Faint** slider, and **Clear** (one undo step). The texture is drawn three by three, the copies dimmed, so a stroke over an edge is seen coming back on the other side; middle drag pans and the wheel zooms, as on the shape canvas.
- **The painter's brushes, unchanged:** the floating brush bar (colour squares, the brush list, Size, Flow, Hardness, Spacing, mirror, pen, smoothing) and the paint tool row sit over the view as they sit over the viewport; the shelf under the canvas shows the painter's Brushes and Colours while the view is open; Attributes shows the box's rows and then the brush's settings (the Paint tab's sections). Strokes use the brush's settings, tip, pen pressure curves, smoothing and spacing (`workbench::box_painting`), each dab painted by **`paint::flat::paint_wrapped_dab`**, whose texels wrap round the edges.
- **Controls (no new ones):** the painter's, over the view: **left drag** paints, **Shift + click** a straight line from the last stroke, **Ctrl + right drag** size and hardness, **Ctrl + left drag** flow, **1** paint, **2** erase (and a pen's eraser end), **5** smudge, **7** blur, **8** the bucket (on a painting it fills the whole painting), **P** picks the colour the box shows, **D** smoothing, **[ ]** size, **L** mirror painting, which on a flat texture flips left and right across its middle (**the UV Editor's rule**, the painter's equivalent on a flat texture). **3** projection, **4** polygon fill and **6** clone need a model's surface or a source on it: on a painting they say what to use instead. **Esc** only cancels a stroke under way; **Tab** goes back to the graph (it waits for a stroke to end).
- **Undo:** one step a stroke (`GraphEdit::SetPainting` when the stroke ends; the view shows the stroke's own image as it goes). The undo history counts only the tiles a stroke changed (`scene::memory`, `Painting::bytes_apart`), so strokes on a 2048 painting stay small.
- **Saved:** file format **107** (`fileio::project::v107`; `v95::BoxV95::painting`): the size and the pixels packed with zstd, read back only as that size's pixels (paid for from the read's memory budget). Graph files and box files read format 3 and write it only when a box holds a painting.
- **Painting on the model in the Material preview: left for later.** It would need the preview's model hit by the brush (the painter's 3D stroke code, a model's UVs mapped onto the texture's repeats at its real-world size and Tiling) and the repeats folded back onto one texture square: not cheap. Noted in the plan.

## Consequences

A graph can hold hand-placed detail that tiles: a painted mask can drive Fill the gaps, a Blend or Mix materials, a painted height can add a dent, a painted colour can mark one stone. The brush, its settings and its keys are the painter's, so nothing new is learnt. Effect brushes that read neighbours (smudge, blur) don't look across the texture's edges, so they work up to an edge rather than over it; paint, erase and the bucket wrap. A heavily painted 2048 painting can make a graph file larger than graph files are read at (16 MB), which then says so. Later milestones can reuse `Painting` for any box holding pixels (stamp images, milestone 22; image to material, milestone 21).
