# 0293. Blend seams

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Follows:** [0179](0179-the-round-brush.md) (painting both sides of a seam), [0203](0203-why-does-this-look-wrong.md) (seams that show), [0261](0261-graphs-in-the-painter.md) (a layer made on another thread, joining the change's undo step), [0028](0028-ask-about-controls.md) (asking about controls)

## Problem

The project lead asked on 2026-11-07 for a way to "mark an edge, and then smoothly blend the left and right side" of a texture seam, as a tool and as a brush (DESIGN.md, *After Phase 12's milestones — seam blending*). Strokes on the model already paint both sides of a UV seam, but paint laid flat on the texture (the UV Editor, UV-aligned strokes, projection, clone, pictures brought in, graph fills) doesn't know where the other side is, so the cut shows as a hard line. This is milestone 1 of the [seam blending plan](../plans/seam-blending.md): the **Blend seams** tool, picking seams, and the **Seam blend** layer. The brush is milestone 2.

## Options

- **Controls** (chosen by the project lead on 2026-11-07, decision 0028): **Blend seams** is **key 9** in Paint mode (it was free there: 1 to 8 are the other paint tools) and a button in the tool row, after the bucket. **Click** a seam in the 3D view to mark it, **Shift + click** marks more, and an **Every seam that shows** button marks them all. No other keys or mouse actions. Shift + click on a seam already marked unmarks it, so a wrong click is put right with the same gesture; **Unmark all** is a button.
- **What a click marks.** (a) One edge: a seam on a real model is many short edges, so it would take many clicks. (b) **The seam line** (chosen): every seam edge between the same two UV pieces joined end to end with the one clicked, which is what a person sees as "the seam". The edges are kept one by one, so Shift + click can still add a second line.
- **How seams are named.** By the object and the two point ids of each edge (`paint::SeamId`). Point ids don't change while the model is moved or posed or its UVs are moved, packed or unwrapped, so the marks survive that; an edge no longer on the model is kept (undo may bring it back), counted and left out, and the panels say how many.
- **Where the result goes** (the project lead): a new **Seam blend** layer on top, made the first time a seam is clicked, holding only the blended texels. Deleting or hiding it takes the blend away; the paint under it is never changed.
- **How the two sides meet.** Each side's texels near the seam are blended towards the matching texels on the other side: the same distance from the seam and the same way along it, found through the two sides' edges on the texture (so pieces turned or mirrored against each other still match, and a side drawn smaller on the texture is sampled at its own scale). On the seam both sides become the half-and-half mix; the blend fades out smoothly over the width. Only texels inside each side's own piece are read and written, so neighbouring pieces and the padding between them are left alone, and pieces drawn on top of each other (mirrored halves sharing texels) are told apart by their triangles, not by the texels. The layer holds the other side's colour as see-through as the blend is strong, so laid over the layers under it with the Normal blend it gives the blended texture. With the realism channels on, roughness, metal, glow and height are blended the same way.
- **Width.** DESIGN.md's draft said centimetres. **Texture pixels each side** (chosen, 1 to 64, 8 to start): seams join pieces of different pixel density, and a seam that shows is a matter of pixels; the slider says what the texture will hold. It can move to centimetres later without changing files' meaning much (a width is a number).
- **Which channels.** DESIGN.md's draft offered a choice; every channel the texture set uses is blended (a seam that shows in colour shows in roughness and height too). A choice can be added if wanted.
- **Every seam that shows:** the seams whose two sides are painted differently enough to see (the same test as "Why does this look wrong?", now shared as `advice::painted_differently`), looked at under the Seam blend layer, added to those marked.
- **Remaking itself, and keeping painting smooth.** The layer keeps a hash of what it was made from (`SeamBlend::made`): its seams and width, the texture size and realism switch, the UVs, where its seams lie on the texture, the settings of the layers under it, and their pixels on just the tiles its seams reach (`seam_blend::tiles_used`; tile hashes are kept by tile, so only tiles that changed are read). Whenever the scene or the drawing changes and no button is held, a layer whose hash differs is made again on another thread, and joins the undo step of the change that asked for it, as graph fills do (decision 0261). Panning, orbiting, painting far from its seams and changing layers above it make nothing; painting near a seam remakes it once the stroke ends.
- **Several texture sets:** a Seam blend layer belongs to one material's texture set and blends the seams of that material only. An edge between two materials isn't a seam of either texture and is left out.
- **Left for milestone 2 and later:** the seam blend brush on the shelf; picking seams in the UV Editor; a seam across two materials; textures that repeat past the 0 to 1 square (trim sheets' repeats) blend only the part on the square.

## Decision

- `paint::seams` (`SeamId`, `Seam`, `SeamSide`, `seams_of`, `find`, `line_of`, `showing`) and `paint::seam_blend` (`SeamBlend` with `seams`, `width` and `made`; `MIN_WIDTH` 1, `MAX_WIDTH` 64, `WIDTH` 8, `MAX_SEAMS`; `BlendShape`, `resolve`, `tiles_used`, `blend`); `Layer::seam_blend` (a made layer); `TextureSet::below`; `composite::flatten_tiles`.
- **File format 111** (`fileio::project::v111`): `LayerV64::seam_blend`, stored only on Seam blend layers; seams out of order are tidied, a width out of range refused. Style presets keep a Seam blend's width, not this project's seams.
- `ui`: `PaintTool::Seams`, `PaintToolButton::Seams` (icon `PaintSeams`), key 9; `workbench/seam_picking.rs` (the tool, the seams shown over the views, the asks), `workbench/seam_blending.rs` (making the layers), `paint_layers/seam_rows.rs` (the rows shared by the Paint tab and the Paint layers panel), `attributes/seam_section.rs`, `viewport/seam_lines.rs`.

## Consequences

Paint laid flat on the texture can be joined across its seams in a click, without painting over it, and the join stays right as the paint under it changes. The layer is ordinary paint once made, so export, previews and Merge down need nothing new. A Seam blend layer below other paint layers doesn't blend them; the manual says to keep it on top. Finding a shape's seams is quick but not free on a heavy shape (it looks at every edge once), so it is done only while the tool is in use or a Seam blend layer exists, and kept while the shape stays the same.
