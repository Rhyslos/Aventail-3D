# 0290. The material health check

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Follows:** [0269](0269-material-extras.md) (the material extras), [0260](0260-preview-and-checks.md) (the seam check and texture budget), [0287](0287-pixel-art-output.md) (pixel art), [0289](0289-trim-sheet-box.md) (trim sheets)

## Problem

Phase 12's twelfth extra (milestone 27, [decision 0269](0269-material-extras.md)): a graph can work out without a single refusal and still make a material that looks poor in a game. The plan asked for warnings in plain words about colours too dark or bright to light well, detail that turns to mush at a distance, and pieces that repeat too obviously, each with a fix. Earlier milestones added more to watch: a Pixelate box that isn't last, pixel-art materials of different pixel sizes, trim sheets whose strips and the project's trim rows disagree, cut strips leaving slivers of a pattern, and the seam check's false alarm when a pattern's line falls on the texture's edge.

## Options

- **Where it shows.**
  - (a) Marks on the canvas: boxes ringed in yellow. Most of these are about the whole material, not one box.
  - (b) **A Health check part in the preview panel** (chosen), under the texture budget and above Use it: the preview is where the material is judged, and the checks read the material Material out is given. Each warning is a line of plain words with buttons under it. Inside your own box it says to go back out (it looks at the whole material).
- **How fixes work.** (a) Words only. (b) **Buttons that make the change** (chosen), as the refusals' fixes do: a setting set, a box put in on a wire (wired both ways, tidied), a box selected and framed, or the project's trim rows made. Each is one undo step; nothing changes until one is clicked. The words still say what else would help, as not every fix is one click.
- **How "too dark" and "too bright" are measured.** The light of each colour (linear, as the eye sums red, green and blue) against the lines physically based painting uses: darker than charcoal (about 30 in 255) and brighter than fresh snow (about 240) for most surfaces, and darker than any real metal (about 180) where the material is metal. A tenth of the material outside the lines is worth saying (a fifth for metal: a blend's edge doesn't count), weighed by opacity on alpha cards. The words say that a game showing the material unlit, as many hand-painted ones do, can keep them.
- **How "mush at a distance" is measured.** Its light and dark (lightness, shaded by its normals, so detail made only of bumps counts) as a spread, then averaged in blocks of 2, 4, 8… pixels as an engine's smaller copies (mipmaps) do. Where half the spread is gone, the block's size in centimetres gives the distance at which it is one pixel on a common screen (1080 pixels tall, a 60° view). Under 50 m (the far side of a courtyard or a big hall) it is said, with the distance and the size of the detail. Considered and left: a share of the repeat (it ignores the real-world size, which is what decides it), or a fixed small copy (same).
- **How "the repeat shows" is measured.** First tried: how much sixteen squares of a repeat differ. Any random pattern of a few blobs passes or fails by chance there. Chosen: **the size of the four slowest waves of light and dark** (once across, down and both diagonals of a repeat). A patch standing out once a repeat makes them strong; many small patches don't. Over about eight steps in a hundred of lightness, it is said, with where the patch stands out (the sixteen squares again: "a lighter patch near the top left"). Not checked on alpha cards and trim sheets, which aren't laid as a repeating surface.
- **Joins (the seam check).** The check now also asks whether a join steps **pixel for pixel like a line inside** (the same line of the pattern one repeat earlier): if one does, the join is a line of the pattern falling on the edge, not a seam. That clears milestone 21's false alarm for patterns made to repeat (a grid, bricks, a mortar line on the edge), while a picture that doesn't tile still shows. A random grey per tile (Tiles' Random) with an edge between two tiles can still be reported, as before. A trim sheet's top-bottom join is left out: its last strip meets its first in a gap, as expected.
- **Trim sheets.** No rows yet (with Strips on the project's rows), strips past the last row, and strips laid by their own heights unlike the project's rows each get a warning, with **Make these the project's trim rows** or a Strips on choice as fixes. A **cut** strip is checked for **slivers**: its material's lines across (rows where the height, or the lightness on a flat material, dips right across) are found, and a strip edge leaving less than half a row of the next one is said, with how much shorter or taller makes it whole, and **Shrink to fit** as the fix. A strip ending inside its only row just shows a narrower piece, which the sheet's own edge finishes: that is fine.
- **Pixel art.** A Pixelate box feeding Material out through other boxes is said (the material isn't pixel art then); a pixel-art material whose pixels a metre differ by more than 1 % from another pixel-art graph in the project is said, with a real-world size making them match.
- **Not in the health check:** what the export check already says (bypassed boxes, boxes following the model, animated boxes): it stays under Use it, where it matters.

## Decision

- `matgraph::health`: `check(registry, graph, evaluation, others) -> Vec<Warning>` (`Warning { concern, words, remedies, boxes }`, `Concern`, `Remedy { words, action }`, `Action::{LookAt, SetSetting, InsertBox (with an optional box feeding it), MakeTrimRows}`, `Action::edits(graph)`, `PixelArt`), with `health/measure.rs` (the measures), `health/looks.rs` (colours, mush, repeats, joins) and `health/trim.rs`.
- `SeamCheck`: `Join::alike` and the line rule; `SeamCheck::across_only` for trim sheets (also used by the Flat preview's seam check).
- `ui::material::health`: the preview's **Health check** part, worked out again only when the pictures, the graph, the registry or the project's other pixel art change, and kept while a quick (small) picture is shown.
- **Fixed on the way:** `connect::boxes_between` looked only at a box's first input, so a colour dragged onto a material plug offered no box between them; it now looks at every input, boxes whose first input fits first (Material from height is offered).
- Starters: Fabric (its weave fades within a few metres), Metal plate and the Trim sheet's bolts and pipe (painted darker than real metal for a hand-painted look) and the Trim sheet in a new project (no rows yet) are warned about on purpose; the others pass. A test keeps it so.
- **No new keys or mouse actions; no file format change.**

## Consequences

The Material mode says what may look wrong in a game before it gets there, and offers the change. The limits are judgements (a common screen, the far side of a courtyard, eight steps in a hundred); they are constants in `health/looks.rs`, easy to tune if the project lead finds the check too eager or too quiet. The distances assume a 1080-pixel screen; on a bigger screen detail holds up further. The check reads the preview's pictures (512 pixels), so detail finer than that is already gone, and the distance said is the longest it could be.
