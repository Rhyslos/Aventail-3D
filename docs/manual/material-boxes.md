# Material boxes

> Every box you can add to a [material graph](material-graphs.md), in plain words, in the order experts use them: **Make**, **From the model**, **Shape**, **Detail**, **Colour**, **Finish**.

Each box does one small job. A graph usually reads left to right in the same order as this page: a **Make** box draws a pattern from nothing, **Shape** boxes change it, a **Colour** box colours it, and a **Finish** box turns it into a whole material. Every box tiles seamlessly, so the material repeats without lines.

## When to use it

- Use this page to find the right box for a job ("I want the edges rounded": **Bevel**; "I want it to look less neat": **Warp**).
- In the app, the same words are in Attributes under **What does this do?** when a box is selected, and **Usually comes next** lists the boxes most often used after it.

## How to use it

1. In the **Material** mode, point at the canvas and press **Space** (or right-click on empty canvas) to open the box search. Type part of a box's name, or pick from the groups.
2. Connect it by dragging from an output dot (right edge) to an input dot (left edge). Wires are coloured by what they carry: grey for a [shape](concepts.md#material-graphs), amber for a [height](concepts.md#material-graphs), blue for a colour, green for a [whole material](concepts.md#material-graphs).
3. Select the box to change its settings in Attributes.

A simple stone-tile material, to see how they fit together:

**Tiles** → **Warp** (the tiles wobble) → **Bevel** (rounded edges) → **Blend** with **Noise** (a bumpy surface) → **Gradient map** (colours) and **Material from height** → **Mix materials** with moss in the gaps → **Material out**.

### Sizes in centimetres

Settings that are a size (a bevel's width, a blur's reach, how far Warp pushes) are given in **centimetres on the finished material**. The graph's [real-world size](concepts.md#real-world-size), set on **Material out**, says how many centimetres the whole texture covers (100 cm to start with). So a bevel 2 cm wide stays 2 cm wide whatever size the texture is worked out at, and if you make the stones bigger by raising the real-world size, the bevel stays the same real width. Numbers that must be whole for the texture to tile (tiles across, cells across, repeats) stay counts.

## The boxes

### Make: patterns from nothing

| Box | What it makes | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Noise** | Soft, cloudy greys: the start of stone, ground, rust and most natural surfaces. | **Scale** (blobs across), **Detail** (finer layers on top), **Roughness** (how strong the finer layers are), **Seed**. |
| **Clouds** | Big, soft, billowing greys, for weathering, dirt and patches of colour. **Billowy** puffs it up like cotton wool; **Ridged** gives sharp ridges like crumpled paper or veins. | **Scale**, **Detail**, **Roughness**, **Look** (Soft, Billowy, Ridged), **Seed**. |
| **Cells** | Irregular cells: cobbles, scales, cracked mud, crystals. **Random** gives each cell its own grey. | **Scale** (cells across), **Jitter** (0 is a neat grid), **Look** (Distance: dark middles; Bumps: domes; Edges: the lines between; Flat: one grey per cell), **Seed**. |
| **Gradient** | Greys running smoothly dark to light: stripes, waves, fading something in, or a soft spot in each repeat. | **Style** (Up and down: no sharp step; Ramp: dark to light then a sharp step; Round: a spot), **Direction** (Across, Down, Diagonal), **Repeats**. |
| **Bricks** | Bricks in rows, every other row moved along, mortar the same width all round. **Random** gives each brick its own grey. | **Rows** (an odd number is made one more, so it tiles), **Bricks per row**, **Mortar**, **Shift**, **Seed**. |
| **Tiles** | A grid of tiles with gaps: floors, walls. **Random** gives each tile its own grey. | **Across**, **Down**, **Gap**, **Shift** (keep Down even with a shift), **Seed**. |
| **Planks** | Rows of boards, each row's ends at a random place. **Random** gives each board a grey; **Grain** gives streaks along each board. | **Boards down**, **Boards per row**, **Gap**, **Grain size**, **Seed**. |
| **Basic shape** | A circle, square, polygon or star, once or in a grid: studs, rivets, holes, dots. | **Shape**, **Size** (how much of each repeat it fills), **Sides** (polygon sides or star points), **Star depth**, **Turn** (degrees), **Repeats**, **Soft edge**. |
| **Shape** | A shape you draw yourself on its own [canvas](shape-canvas.md): a leaf, a blade, a rune. Double-click it (or **Open the canvas** in Attributes) to draw. It gives the shape as a sharp **Mask** at any texture size, a rounded **Height** (puffed up, each piece as high as you set it), its **Outline** and its **Colours** (each piece in its own colour, over black). Build it from many pieces and [linked copies](concepts.md#linked-copy): a twig of leaves. Give shapes jagged, serrated, wavy or torn [edges](concepts.md#edge-randomiser), let every copy differ, and draw lines for [veins](concepts.md#vein) (grooves or ridges in the Height, darker or lighter in the Colours). | **Puff** (how far in from the edge, in cm, the shape rises to its full height: more is rounder; 0 is a flat top), **Height** (how high the top is), **Outline** (how wide the outline and drawn lines are, in cm). |
| **Image** | A photo or a painting of your own, the start of a [material from a photo](material-graphs.md#from-a-photo-to-a-material). Click **Bring in a picture…** in Attributes (or double-click the box) and pick a PNG, JPEG, TGA or BMP file. It gives the picture's square part as a **Colour**, and how light it is as a **Lightness** (a height). A photo doesn't repeat cleanly and has the light it was taken in: **Make into material** in Attributes adds the boxes that fix that. With no picture yet, it says so. | **Square size** (how much of the picture the square takes: 1 is the biggest square that fits), **Left to right** and **Top to bottom** (where the square sits in a picture that isn't square). |

### From the model: following the model you paint

These boxes bring in the model's own [baked maps](baking-maps.md) when the graph is used in the painter, as a [graph fill](painting-textures.md#graph-fills). That is what makes a graph a [smart material](concepts.md#smart-material): "moss in the crevices" or "lighter on the edges" then follows the model being painted, not a pattern. They have no settings: use **Levels** after them to choose how much shows, and **Mix materials** (its **Where**) or **Blend** to use them.

In the Material mode there is no model, so they show a **stand-in**: a field of rounded bumps, four across, worked out the same way (edges round each bump, creases between them). It lets you build and check the graph before you paint with it; in the painter the real model replaces it.

| Box | What it gives | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| **Curvature** | The model's edges and creases: light on outer edges, dark in creases, middle grey where flat. | **Curvature** (a height), **Edges** and **Creases** (shapes, white where they are). Edges for worn or lightened edges, Creases for dirt and moss. |
| **Occlusion** | How open the surface is to the sky: white where open, darker in crevices and corners light hardly reaches. | **Occlusion** (a height). |
| **Position** | Where on the model: black at its bottom to white at its top, and the same across and from front to back. | **Up**, **Across**, **Front to back** (heights). Up for dirt rising from the ground, or snow on top. |
| **Thickness** | How thin the model is: white where thin (leaves, ears, fins), dark where thick. | **Thickness** (a height). |
| **Facing up** | How much the surface faces up: white on top, black on the sides and underneath. | **Facing up** (a height). Dust, snow and moss settle there. |

How a graph with these is worked out on the model: the parts of the graph that don't use them make the material's repeat, as always, at its real-world size; from the first box that uses the model onwards, the graph works on the model's whole texture, with the repeat laid across it the way the preview lays it. So sizes in centimetres stay right in both parts.

### Shape: changing a shape or height

| Box | What it does | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Blur** | Softens a height, smoothing sharp edges and small bumps. | **Radius** (cm). |
| **Warp** | Pushes a height about so straight lines wobble: neat tiles become hand-cut stone. With a height in **By**, things move the way it slopes upwards; with nothing there, its own noise moves them. | **Amount** (cm), **Scale** (its own wobbles across), **Seed**. |
| **Levels** | Changes how dark and light a height is: crisper or softer edges, or turned upside down. | **Darkest**, **Lightest**, **Middle**, **Turn upside down**. |
| **Edge finder** | Draws a shape's outline as a soft white line: worn edges, grout, seams. | **Width** (cm), **Side** (Inside, Outside, Both). |
| **Bevel** | Turns a black-and-white shape into a height with rounded edges, as if each tile were cut with a rounded, sloped or stepped edge. | **Width** (cm: how far in the edge reaches), **Roundness** (softens the edge's corners), **Height** (how high the top is), **Edge** (Round, Sharp, Steps), **Steps**. |
| **Transform** | Moves, turns or flips a height, a colour or a whole material; what goes off one edge comes back on the other. | **Move across**, **Move down** (shares of the texture), **Turn** (quarter turns only: other angles would break the tiling), **Flip left to right**, **Flip top to bottom**. |
| **Repeat** | Copies a height, a colour or a whole material in a grid, smaller: one stud becomes a sheet of studs. | **Across**, **Down**, **Shift** (every other row; keep Down even), **Vary** (each copy a little darker, and a height lower, at random), **Seed**. |
| **Blend** | Mixes two heights, Top over Bottom, through a shape in **Where** if given. | **Mode** (Mix, Add, Subtract, Multiply, Lighter, Darker), **Amount**. |
| **Slope blur** | [Smears](concepts.md#slope-blur) a height, a colour or a whole material along the slopes of another height (**By**), as rain wears stone, paint runs and sand drifts. Steep places smear further, flat ones not at all. With nothing in **By**, its own noise gives the slopes. | **Distance** (cm), **Mode** (Smear blurs along the slope; Wear down eats into what is high; Build up spreads it), **Direction** (Downhill: what is higher slides down over lower places; Uphill), **Steps** (more is smoother and slower), **Scale** (its own hills across), **Seed**. |
| **Make seamless** | Makes a picture that doesn't repeat cleanly (a photo) [tile](concepts.md#seamless-tiling) without a visible join. Near its edges it shows a copy moved half a texture along, whose joins are in the middle where the picture itself is shown, and blends the two so the joins vanish (keeping the contrast, so the blend doesn't look washed out). Takes a height, a colour or a whole material. Its Attributes show the [seam check](material-graphs.md#the-seam-check). | **Blend width** (how far in from each edge the copies are blended, as a share of the texture), **Wobble** (how much the line where they meet wanders; 0 is straight), **Seed**. |
| **Guess height** | Guesses a height from a colour picture: light parts are taken as high and dark ones as low, as on most stone, bark or ground, where what sticks out catches the light. Only changes smaller than **Detail size** count, so a shadow across the photo isn't taken for a slope. | **Detail size** (about the size of one stone or plank, as a share of the texture), **Big shapes** (adds back some plain lightness, so whole stones stand up), **Smoothing** (so grain doesn't make spikes), **Invert** (dark is high). |
| **Guess roughness** | Guesses how rough or shiny each part of a colour picture is: busy, grainy parts (rough rock, bark) are taken as matt, smooth even ones (polished or wet patches) as shiny. Gives greys from **Shiniest** to **Roughest**, for Material from height's **Roughness**. | **Shiniest**, **Roughest** (0 is a mirror, 1 fully matt), **Detail size** (about the size of the grain that makes a part rough), **Invert** (busy parts shiny). |

**Transform, Repeat and Slope blur on colours and materials.** Each has three inputs, **Height**, **Colour** and **Material**, and an output for each. Plug in any of them, or two or three at once to move them together, and take each from its own output: a coloured pattern turned a quarter, a finished stone material copied four times smaller. Why three plugs and not one: a wire's colour says what it carries, and each output carries one kind. A box needs at least one input plugged in, and an output whose input is empty gives nothing (a box wired to it says so). A whole material moves every map the same way, and its bumps turn with it: the light still falls on the right side of each brick after a quarter turn, and smaller copies have steeper slopes, as smaller bumps of the same height would. Graphs made before keep working as they were: their wires go to the Height plugs.

### Detail: small things on top

| Box | What it does | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Scatter** | Places copies of a shape, a colour or a whole material at random or in a grid (it [scatters](concepts.md#scatter) them), each with its own size, turn, height and colour: pebbles, grass tufts, scales, rivets. With nothing plugged in, it places round pebbles. | **Arrange** (At random, In a grid), **Spacing** (cm between copies), **Size** (cm across the biggest copy), **Size varies**, **Turn** (degrees), **Turn varies** (degrees either way), **Height varies**, **Colour varies**, **Keep apart**, **Loose** and **Shift rows** (a grid only), **Seed**. |
| **Stamps** | Scatters small pictures of your own as [stamps](concepts.md#stamp): a rivet, a leaf, a rune, a painted flower. Click **Add a stamp…** in Attributes (or double-click the box) and pick a PNG, JPEG, TGA or BMP; add up to 16. Each copy picks one of them at random and gets its own size, turn, height and colour, placed the same ways as Scatter's. With no stamps yet, it says so. | Scatter's **Arrange**, **Spacing**, **Size** (cm along a stamp's longer side), **Size varies**, **Turn**, **Turn varies**, **Height varies**, **Colour varies**, **Keep apart** (on to start with), **Loose**, **Shift rows**, plus **Height from** (Its outline, Its lightness), **Puff** (0 a flat step, 1 a dome), **Seed**. Each stamp in the list has **How often** (1 to 10) and **Take off**. |

| **Cracks** | Carves a network of thin, wandering cracks into a height: dried mud, old plaster, cracked stone or paint. With nothing plugged in, into flat white. **Cracks** gives the lines alone, to colour them or fill them with dirt; with a shape in **Where**, cracks only show where it is white. | **Piece size** (cm: the pieces between the cracks), **Width** (cm, at a crack's widest), **Amount** (how many of the pieces' borders crack), **Wiggle** (0 is straight lines), **Depth**, **Seed**. |
| **Chips** | Takes bites out of a shape's edges, as on old bricks, worn tiles or chipped paint. Gives the chipped **Shape**, a **Height** with the chips cut down (from the height in **Height**, or the shape itself), and the **Chips** alone. | **Chip size** (cm across a chip), **Reach** (cm: how far in the biggest bites), **Amount**, **Depth** (1 cuts right to the bottom), **Seed**. |
| **Scratches** | Scores thin scratches into a height, each tapering to a point: worn metal, scuffed wood, old paint. With nothing plugged in, into flat white. **Scratches** gives the lines alone, to lighten them on a colour; with a shape in **Where**, scratches only start where it is white. | **Spacing** (cm: about one scratch in each square this wide), **Length** (cm), **Length varies**, **Width** (cm), **Direction** (degrees, clockwise from across), **Direction varies** (degrees either way: small for brushed metal, 180 for every way), **Strength varies**, **Depth**, **Seed**. |
| **Fill the gaps** | Fills a height's low places up to a level, as moss, dirt, sand or grout settle between stones, tiles and planks. Gives where the filling is (**Gaps**), the height filled, and, when a colour or a whole material is plugged in, the same with the filling's colour (and roughness) laid into the gaps. | **Level** (0 none, 1 everything), **Softness** (how gently it fades up the sides), **Patchy**, **Patch size** (cm), **Filling colour**, **Colour varies**, **Filling roughness** (for a material), **Seed**. |
| **Painted** | A box you [paint](concepts.md#painted-box) on with the painter's brushes, for things placed by hand: a crack just there, moss in one corner, one darker stone. Right-click it → **Paint on it** (or double-click it) opens the [painting view](material-graphs.md#painting-inside-a-graph). **Kind** picks what it paints, and only that output gives anything: a **Mask** (white where the paint is light, black where nothing is painted), a **Height** (mid grey where nothing is painted) or a **Colour**. Plug something into its input of that kind to paint over it. | **Kind** (Mask, Height, Colour), **Blend** (Over covers what is under; Add lightens it; Subtract darkens it; Multiply darkens it by the paint), **Strength** (0 none of the paint, 1 all of it). |

**Cracks, Chips, Scratches or Fill the gaps?** They all add wear to a height. Cracks and Scratches are lines (cracks join up into pieces; scratches are short and loose), Chips bites into the edges of a shape, and Fill the gaps adds something on top, in the low places. Each cuts or fills a height you plug in and also gives its marks on their own, so you can colour them: lighter scratches on metal, dark dirt in cracks. Fill the gaps fills with a colour; to fill with a whole second material (real moss, with its own bumps), use **Mix materials** by height instead.

**Painted or a pattern?** The other Detail boxes spread their marks over the whole texture by a seed; **Painted** puts them exactly where you brush them. Use both: Cracks everywhere, then a Painted mask into a Blend's **Where** so a big crack shows only where you painted. With a mask or a height, the brush's colour counts by how light it is: paint white to add, black to take away (or use the eraser, which takes the paint off and shows what is under again). The paint is kept at its own resolution (256 to 2048 pixels across) and scaled to the size the graph is worked out at, wrapping round the edges, so it [tiles seamlessly](concepts.md#seamless-tiling) like every other box.

**What goes in.** **Shape** is the outline of one copy: a Basic shape for round pebbles or rivets, a star, or a shape you made. A height works too, and its greys become each copy's height (a Bevel's rounded stone, say). **Colour** and **Material** are copied as they are: with no Shape, each copy is the whole picture, made small and square. **Where** says where copies may go: they only land on its white parts, and on its greys only some do (paint a gradient in to make pebbles thin out). With nothing plugged in at all, Scatter places round pebbles, so you can drop one in and see what it does.

**What comes out.** **Material** and **Colour** give the copies of what was plugged in (black between them); an output whose input is empty gives nothing. **Height** is the copies' heights, **Copies** is white wherever a copy is (plug it into Mix materials' **Where** to lay the copies over a ground), and **Random** gives each copy its own grey, to colour each pebble differently with a Gradient map.

**How copies sit.** Where copies overlap, the higher one is on top, as in a heap of pebbles; turn on **Keep apart** to stop them overlapping (copies that would are left out, so there may be fewer). Copies reach over the texture's edges and come back on the other side, so the texture still [tiles seamlessly](concepts.md#seamless-tiling). The same **Seed** always places the same copies. **Spacing** and **Size** are centimetres: at random, there is about one copy in each square Spacing wide, so on a 2 m texture with 20 cm Spacing, about a hundred. In a grid, Spacing is fitted to a whole number of copies across, so the grid tiles. For fish scales or roof tiles: In a grid, **Shift rows** 0.5, **Turn varies** 0 and **Size** a little more than **Spacing**, so the rows overlap. A material's bumps turn with each copy, and smaller copies have steeper slopes.

**How many.** One Scatter places at most 16 384 copies, and never so many big ones that they would pile more than about 32 deep: past that, the box leaves the rest out, so it never takes minutes to work out.

#### Stamps: your own pictures as copies

**Why use it.** Scatter copies what you plug into it, so every copy is the same shape. **Stamps** copies small *pictures* you bring in, with all their colours and detail, and mixes several: three different rivets, oak and maple leaves, a set of runes. Use Scatter for shapes you build in the graph, Stamps for pictures you drew or found elsewhere.

**Bringing pictures in.** Choose the Stamps box, then **Add a stamp…** in Attributes (double-clicking the box does the same) and pick a picture. A **PNG with a see-through background** works best: the see-through parts give each stamp its outline and soft edges. A picture with no see-through parts (a JPEG, say) is stamped as its whole square, and the note says so. Pictures bigger than 512 pixels are made smaller (a stamp only covers a small part of the texture), and kept in the project, so you don't need the file afterwards. Each stamp in the list shows its picture and name, **How often** it is picked (a stamp at 3 turns up about three times as often as one at 1, which makes rare stamps easy: one cracked rivet among many) and **Take off**. Every change is one undo step.

**What comes out.** **Colour** gives the stamps in their own colours, lying over whatever is plugged into **Under** (black without it). **Height** gives their heights, from **Height from**: **Its outline** makes each stamp flat on top, as high as it is solid; **Its lightness** makes light parts higher, so a drawn leaf keeps its veins. **Puff** rounds the height up from each stamp's edge, like something pressed into clay: 0 is a sharp step, 1 a dome (good for rivets). **Copies** is white wherever a stamp is, by its see-through parts, and **Random** gives each copy its own grey. Plug a whole material into **Material** to get it back with the stamps **pressed on**: their colours on top, standing up from its height, with bumps at their edges, its shine and metal kept. Plug a shape into **Where** to say where stamps may land, as with Scatter.

**How stamps sit.** **Keep apart** is on to start with, so stamps sit side by side; turn it off to let them pile up, the one on top chosen at random and its soft edge laid over the ones under it. Stamps reach over the texture's edges and come back on the other side, so it still [tiles seamlessly](concepts.md#seamless-tiling), and the same **Seed** always gives the same stamps in the same places. Adding a stamp or changing How often changes which picture each copy shows, never where the copies are. For letters and runes that should stay upright, set **Turn varies** to 0.

**Stamps come along.** Stamps are saved in the project, in graph files and in your own boxes, and come along when you copy the box, even into another tab.

### Colour

| Box | What it does | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour fill** | One colour everywhere. | **Colour**. |
| **Gradient map** | Colours a height: each grey takes the colour at the same place along a gradient, black at its left end, white at its right. The quickest way to make colour follow shape: dark in the cracks, light on top. | **Colours**: the gradient, drawn as a strip. Each colour has a swatch to pick it, a slider for where it sits (0 for black, 1 for white) and **×** to take it out; **Add a colour** puts one in the widest gap (2 to 8 colours). |
| **Hue and saturation** | Changes a colour picture's hues, how colourful it is, and how light. With a shape in **Where**, only the white parts change. | **Hue** (degrees round the colour wheel), **Saturation** (0 grey, 1 as it is, 2 twice as colourful), **Lightness**. |
| **Palette map** | Colours a height with one of the project's [palettes](concepts.md#palette) (the ones in the Paint mode's colour picker), so every material keeps to the game's colours. **Steps** gives each band of greys one swatch (dark greys the darkest swatch, with **Order** at Dark to light). **Smooth** blends from swatch to swatch, like a gradient made of the palette. **Nearest** turns each colour plugged into **Colour** into the palette's closest swatch (by how alike they look), which is how pixel art keeps to a few colours. With only a colour plugged in, Steps and Smooth use how light it is; with only a height, Nearest snaps its greys. Change the palette in the painter and every Palette map using it changes too, in the Material mode and on painted models. | **Palette** (the list shows each palette's swatches; **First palette** follows whichever is first in the colour picker's list), **Mode** (Steps, Smooth, Nearest), **Order** (As listed, Reversed, Dark to light, Light to dark), **First swatch** and **Last swatch** (use only part of the palette, counting from 1 as the colour picker shows it), **Dither** (mixes neighbouring swatches in a fine checked pattern where one meets the next, for Steps and Nearest: a retro, pixel-art look). |
| **Colour to shape** | Turns a colour picture into a black-and-white shape, so boxes that need a shape (Bevel, Edge finder, a Where plug) can use it. A colour on its own has no inside or outside: this box decides one. **Height** gives the same greys as a height. | **By** (Lightness, or Close to a colour), **Colour**, **Threshold**, **Softness**. |

| **Brush strokes** | Repaints a colour (or a whole material's colour) as overlapping strokes of a brush, for a hand-painted look: fine detail becomes dabs of paint, each a little lighter or darker, with faint streaks from the bristles. Its **Height** is the strokes' paint ridges, to blend into a height. | **Length**, **Width** (cm, each stroke), **Size varies**, **Direction** (degrees), **Direction varies**, **Detail kept** (how much of the colour under a stroke shows through: 0 is flat dabs), **Colour varies**, **Bristles**, **Seed**. |
| **Edge highlight** | Lightens the raised edges of a height and darkens its hollows, as hand painters pick out the edges of stones, planks and bricks. Plug the height into **Height** and the colour (or a whole material) to light. **Edges** gives the highlight alone. | **Width** (cm: how far in from an edge), **Amount**, **Highlight colour**, **Darken hollows**. |
| **Directional light** | Paints light and shade into a colour from a height, as if a sun shone across the material: warm where it reaches, cool in the shade, with shadows from the bumps. It is the painter's [light and shade](painting-textures.md#light-and-shade) helper ([painted-in light](concepts.md#painted-in-light)) for a material, with the same settings and the same turn from light to shade. With no colour plugged in, it gives the light and shade colours alone. **Light** gives how lit each place is. | **Around** (degrees: 0 from the right, 90 from the top of the texture), **Sun height** (degrees), **Depth** (cm from black to white in the height: deeper makes steeper slopes and longer shadows), **Light colour**, **Shade colour**, **Softness** (0 is a hard, toon line), **Warmth**, **Shadows**, **Amount**. |
| **Toon bands** | Lights a colour (or a whole material) in a few flat tones, as [cel-shaded](concepts.md#toon-shading) games and cartoons do: the light is worked out as Directional light works it out (same sun, same slopes), then cut into **Bands**, each one flat tone from the **Shade colour** to the **Light colour**, multiplied over the colour. Plug a height into **Height**, or only a whole material: its own bumps are used. With no colour plugged in, it gives the tones alone. **Bands** (the output) tells which band each place is in, as a height: black the darkest, white the lightest. | **Around**, **Sun height** (degrees, as in Directional light), **Depth** (cm), **Bands** (2 to 8), **Softness** (0 a hard line between bands; more blurs only the lines), **Colours** (Shade and light, or Palette: every colour snapped to the closest of a [palette](concepts.md#palette)'s swatches; with no colour plugged in, each band is one swatch, dark to light), **Shade colour**, **Light colour**, **Palette**, **Shadows** (bumps cast shadows into the darkest band), **Amount**. |
| **Painted highlight** | Paints a stroke of light along only the edges that face a light, as a painter adds a rim or top light to stones, planks and leaves. An edge is where the height stands above what is round it; which way it faces comes from its slope, so the stroke follows the top edges with the light from above (90), the right edges with it from the right (0). **Highlight** gives the stroke alone, to use as a shape elsewhere. | **Around** (where the light comes from, degrees: 90 from the top of the texture), **Spread** (0 only the edges facing it straight on, 1 every edge), **Width** (cm, how far in from an edge), **Softness** (0 a crisp painted stroke, 1 a gentle glow), **Highlight colour**, **Amount**. |
| **Colour steps** | [Posterises](concepts.md#posterise): cuts colours into a few flat steps, for a cel or poster look. **Lightness only** keeps each colour's hue and cuts only how light it is (by eye), so a material keeps its colours in flat tones; **Each channel** cuts red, green and blue apart, which flattens and shifts colours as a poster print does. Plug in a colour or a whole material (its colour changes, the rest stays); a height comes out in terraces. | **Steps** (2 to 32), **Mode** (Each channel, Lightness only), **Dither** (mixes neighbouring steps in a fine checked pattern where they meet), **Snap to a palette** (every colour then becomes the closest of the palette's swatches), **Palette**. |
| **Even out light** | Takes the light and shadow a photo was taken in out of it: one side lighter than the other, or patches of shade under a tree, which would show as stripes and blotches when the texture repeats. It blurs the picture's lightness until only the big patches of light are left, and evens those out, keeping the small detail; the game lights the material instead. | **Strength** (0 none, 1 all of it), **Light patch size** (how big a patch of light must be to be taken out: bigger keeps more of the picture's own dark and light parts), **Edges** (**Tiling**: the picture already repeats cleanly; **Photo**: straight from an Image box, before Make seamless). |

**Palettes, and why.** A game looks like one world when its materials share a few colours. A [palette](concepts.md#palette) kept with the project (made in the [colour picker](painting-textures.md#colours-in-the-shelf), or one of the starter palettes) is that set of colours; Palette map makes a material use it, and when you change a colour in the palette every material using it follows. To keep a Gradient map's look but snap it to the palette, put a Palette map set to **Nearest** after it.

**When the palette isn't there.** A Palette map names its palette, so if the palette is deleted or renamed, or the box came from another project, the box can't run: it says so on the canvas and in Attributes, and offers the project's other palettes as buttons (or, in a project with none yet, **Add the starter palettes to this project**). Boxes pasted from another project bring the palettes they name along, unless this project already has one by that name: then the box uses this project's colours, which is the point of a palette.

**Painted-in light, and why.** In a hand-painted game the light is often part of the texture: the tops of stones lighter and warmer, their sides cooler and darker, so the material reads well under flat or no lighting. Directional light does that for a whole material in one box, matching the painter's helper so a material and a model painted by hand look lit by the same sun. Keep the sun the same across a project's materials. For realistic materials, leave it out: the game's own lights do this.

#### A cel or hand-painted look

**Why flat tones.** A smooth fade from light to shade looks real; a few flat tones with crisp lines between them look drawn, the way cartoons, comics and [cel-shaded](concepts.md#toon-shading) games look. Painting those tones into the texture means the look holds under any lighting in the game, even none. Three boxes do it, usually one after another:

1. **Toon bands** turns the height's light and shade into Bands flat tones. Two bands is the boldest cartoon look; three or four read better on stones and wood. Keep **Softness** low for crisp lines, and the sun the same across a project's materials (the same **Around** and **Sun height** as your Directional light boxes and the painter's light and shade).
2. **Painted highlight** adds the painter's last touch: a light stroke along the edges facing the light. Use the same direction as the sun in Toon bands (Around 240 there is light from the lower left; 90 from the top is the usual top light).
3. **Colour steps** flattens what is left of the colour's own shading into a few steps, so the whole material reads as painted in flat colour. **Lightness only** keeps your colours; **Snap to a palette** keeps them to the game's.

With a [palette](concepts.md#palette), Toon bands' **Colours** at **Palette** and Colour steps' **Snap to a palette** both keep the tones to the game's colours, as Palette map does. A box naming a palette the project hasn't got says so and offers the project's others, as Palette map does.

### Moving: boxes that change over time

These four boxes make a material move: lava creeping, a river, a glowing ember, a conveyor belt. They move in the Material mode's preview when its **player** plays ([Animated materials](material-graphs.md#animated-materials)); everywhere else (exports, the painter, a shape given the material) the moment chosen with **Bake this moment** is baked, because a texture is a still picture. Each can sit anywhere in a graph: everything after it moves too, and everything before it is worked out once and kept.

| Box | What it does | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | The time as it plays, as a flat grey, to drive other boxes: **Loop** rises from black to white and starts again, **Wave** rises and falls smoothly. Plug one into a Blend's **Where** to fade one pattern into another, or into Levels or a Gradient map. | **Period** (seconds one loop lasts). |
| **Scroll** | Slides a height, a colour or a whole material across the texture: a conveyor belt, a waterfall, clouds drifting. What slides off one edge comes back on the other, so it always tiles. | **Direction** (degrees: 0 right, 90 up), **Speed** (cm a second, at the real-world size), **Loops** (on: back at the start every **Loop length** seconds without a jump; the way it moves is nudged so a whole number of repeats goes by, at least one), **Loop length** (seconds). |
| **Pulse** | Brightens a colour (a material's colour) and strengthens a height, then lets them go, over and over: glowing embers, a heartbeat, a blinking light. With nothing plugged in, it gives the strength as a flat grey. | **Rate** (pulses a second), **Shape** (Smooth: rises and falls gently; Sharp: a flash that fades; Steps: holds at even levels), **Steps** (how many levels; 2 blinks on and off), **Lowest**, **Highest** (1 as it is, 2 twice as bright or high, 0 dark or flat). |
| **Flow** | Drags a height, a colour or a whole material along, each part its own way: lava, rivers, slime. It flows in **Direction**, or downhill along a height plugged into **Downhill on**, or the way a [flow map](concepts.md#flow-map) plugged into **Flow map** says. | **Direction** (when nothing steers it), **Speed** (cm a second), **Stretch time** (seconds it is dragged before fading into a fresh copy). |

**Why Flow fades between two copies.** A picture dragged along for ever smears into long streaks. So Flow drags it for **Stretch time** seconds and starts again, but a restart would jump. Instead two copies take turns half a stretch apart: the picture fades from one to the other, and each copy starts again only while it is fully faded out, so you never see a reset. Games' water shaders do the same. Shorter stretches keep the picture crisp; longer ones let it travel further before it fades.

**Speeds are real.** Scroll's and Flow's **Speed** is in centimetres a second on the finished material (through Material out's **Real-world size**), so a river flowing 20 cm a second flows at that speed whatever size its texture is.

### Finish: whole materials

| Box | What it does | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Material from height** | Turns a height into a whole material: works out the normal (how light catches the bumps), occlusion (darker in dips) and curvature (lighter on ridges), and adds colour, roughness and metal. Plug greys into **Roughness** (Guess roughness, or any height) for a roughness that changes across the material. | **Colour** (when nothing is plugged into Colour), **Depth**, **Roughness** (when nothing is plugged into Roughness), **Metal**, **Occlusion**. |
| **Mix materials** | Lays one whole material over another, mixing every map at once. **By shape**: Top shows where Where is white (everywhere with nothing there). **By height**: Top shows where it stands higher than Bottom, as moss fills cracks or snow sits on stones. | **By** (Shape, Height), **Amount**, **Rise** (lifts Top, so more or less of it shows), **Softness**. |
| **Opacity** | Makes a material see-through outside a shape: an [alpha card](concepts.md#alpha-card) for leaves, grass, fences and chains. Plug the Shape box's **Mask** into **Opacity**: white stays solid, black is cut away. The light passing through thin parts from behind is its [translucency](concepts.md#translucency), less where the material is thick (**Thickness**, or its own height when nothing is plugged in), so veins and stems stay darker. The colour under the cut-away parts is filled from the nearest solid colour, so no dark fringe shows round the edges in a game. | **Translucency** (0 none, no translucency map), **Thick parts block light**. |
| **Material out** | The graph's result: the material plugged in here is what the graph makes. One per graph. | **Real-world size** (cm one repeat covers). |

## Settings

Each box's settings are in the tables above. Two settings appear on many boxes:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Seed** | Another number gives another pattern of the same look. It is mixed with the graph's own seed, so changing the graph's seed changes every pattern at once. |
| A size in **cm** | A length on the finished material, through Material out's **Real-world size**. |

Sliders, colours and switches can also be marked **Change in the game** in Attributes (a box selected): see [Animated materials](material-graphs.md#animated-materials). Sliders with a wide range (a speed, seconds, the real-world size) slow down near their small end, so small values are easy to pick.

## Tips and common problems

- **Problem:** Bevel makes everything one flat white. **Why:** the gaps in its shape are thinner than a pixel at the size being worked out, so there is no outside to round towards. **Fix:** widen the gaps (Tiles' **Gap**, Bricks' **Mortar**), or look at a bigger picture; the canvas works pictures out at 256 pixels or more.
- **Problem:** a wire from a colour won't go into Bevel. **Why:** Bevel needs to know what is inside and outside, and a colour has neither. **Fix:** the note offers **Put a Colour to shape box between them**: click it.
- **Problem:** after Tiles or Repeat with a **Shift**, there is a line at the top of the texture. **Why:** with an odd number of rows, the first and last rows are both unshifted. **Fix:** keep **Down** even (Bricks makes its rows even for you).
- **Tip:** to make each tile a little different, plug Tiles' (or Bricks', Planks', Cells') **Random** into a Blend with a small Amount, or into a Gradient map for a different colour per tile.
- **Problem:** Scatter makes fewer copies than Spacing says. **Why:** **Keep apart** leaves out copies that would overlap, and **Where** leaves out copies on its dark parts. **Fix:** make **Size** smaller, or turn off **Keep apart**.
- **Problem:** Scatter's copies look cut off at their sides. **Why:** each copy is the whole picture plugged in, made small; a shape that touches the picture's edges gets square sides. **Fix:** make the shape smaller in its picture (Basic shape's **Size** below 1).
- **Tip:** for grass tufts, plug a thin star or a hand-made blade into Scatter's **Shape**, with **Turn varies** at 180 and **Size varies** about 0.5.
- **Problem:** Stamps come out as squares. **Why:** the picture has no see-through parts (a JPEG, or a PNG saved with a solid background), so each stamp is its whole square. **Fix:** save the picture as a PNG with a see-through background in your painting program, and add it again.
- **Problem:** Stamps makes fewer stamps than Spacing says. **Why:** **Keep apart** (on to start with) leaves out stamps that would overlap. **Fix:** make **Size** smaller than **Spacing**, or turn **Keep apart** off.
- **Problem:** **Add a stamp…** is greyed out. **Why:** a Stamps box holds at most 16 stamps. **Fix:** take one off, or use a second Stamps box and mix the two.
- **Tip:** rivets on a metal plate: draw one rivet (a light dot with a darker rim) on a see-through background, add it to Stamps, set **Arrange** to In a grid, **Puff** about 0.8, and plug your metal material into **Material**.
- **Problem:** Chips makes no chips. **Why:** it needs an outside to bite from; with gaps thinner than a pixel (small pictures), the shape is all inside. **Fix:** widen the shape's gaps, or look at a bigger picture.
- **Problem:** a Painted box's Colour (or Height) output won't give anything; the box after it shows a **!**. **Why:** only the output of the box's **Kind** gives the paint. **Fix:** change **Kind** in Attributes (or on the painting view's bar), or connect the output of the kind it paints.
- **Problem:** the paint on a Painted mask looks grey, not white. **Why:** a mask takes the brush's colour by how light it is, and the brush's **Flow** lays it on only partly each stroke. **Fix:** pick white (or a light colour) and raise **Flow**, or go over it again.
- **Tip:** to put moss only where you want it: Fill the gaps on your stones' height, then a Blend with the plain height at the bottom, Fill the gaps' height at the top and a **Painted** mask in **Where**; brush the mask where the moss should grow.
- **Problem:** Guess height made the gaps between stones high and the stones low. **Why:** in that photo the stones are darker than the gaps (dark rock, light mortar). **Fix:** tick **Invert**.
- **Problem:** Guess height looks noisy, every grain a spike. **Why:** grain in the photo counts as detail too. **Fix:** raise **Smoothing**, or follow it with a **Blur**; a bigger **Detail size** gives more weight to whole stones.
- **Problem:** after Make seamless, a brick wall shows two rows at once in a band near the edges. **Why:** the blend mixes the picture with a copy moved half a texture, and a strong regular pattern doesn't line up with itself there. **Fix:** narrow **Blend width**, or choose the Image box's square part (Square size, Left to right) so the picture holds a whole number of bricks across and down.
- **Problem:** Even out light made the material flat and grey. **Why:** **Light patch size** is so small it evens out the stones themselves. **Fix:** raise **Light patch size**, or lower **Strength**.
- **Problem:** Directional light hardly changes the colour. **Why:** the height's slopes are gentle at its **Depth**. **Fix:** raise **Depth**, or lower **Sun height** so the light comes in from the side.
- **Tip:** for a hand-painted stone: Bevel → Gradient map → **Brush strokes** → **Edge highlight** → **Directional light**, then Material from height.
- **Tip:** for a cel-shaded stone: Bevel → Gradient map → **Toon bands** (Height from the Bevel) → **Painted highlight** (Height from the Bevel) → **Colour steps**, then Material from height.
- **Problem:** Toon bands shows only one tone. **Why:** the height's slopes are too gentle at its **Depth** to turn any place away from the sun, so everything lands in one band. **Fix:** raise **Depth**, lower **Sun height**, or add a band.
- **Problem:** Painted highlight shows almost nothing. **Why:** it paints edges, and a soft height (Clouds, Noise) has hardly any: nowhere stands clearly above what is round it. **Fix:** use it on a height with edges (Bevel, Tiles, Cells), put **Levels** before it to steepen the height, widen **Width**, or raise **Spread**.
- **Problem:** Toon bands or Painted highlight shows a **!** saying it needs a height or a material. **Why:** with only a colour plugged in, it can't tell which way anything faces. **Fix:** plug the height the colour was made from into **Height**.
- **Problem:** Colour steps at **Lightness only** turned a very bright colour paler. **Why:** a strong colour can't be shown at every lightness; to keep the lightness asked for, it is made a little less strong. **Fix:** use more **Steps**, or **Each channel**.
- **Tip:** brushed metal: **Scratches** with **Direction varies** at 0 to 5 and a short **Spacing**; a scuffed floor: **Direction varies** 180.
- **Tip:** Warp before Bevel makes hand-cut stones; Warp after Bevel makes worn, soft ones.
- **Tip:** try Gradient map with dark colours on the left and warm, light ones on the right for a hand-painted look.
- **Tip:** for pixel art, work the material out small, then Palette map with **Nearest** and a little **Dither** after the colour, so every pixel is one of the game's colours.
- **Problem:** Palette map's colours came out in the wrong order (light in the cracks). **Why:** **Order** is **As listed** and the palette lists light colours first. **Fix:** choose **Dark to light**, or **Reversed**.
- **Tip:** lava: Clouds → **Flow** (Downhill on its own height, or a slow Direction) → Gradient map from dark red to yellow → Material from height, with a **Pulse** (Smooth, Rate 0.3) on the colour for a slow glow.
- **Problem:** Scroll moves faster (or in a slightly different direction) than its settings say. **Why:** with **Loops** on, it is nudged so a whole number of repeats goes by each loop, so the loop has no jump. **Fix:** turn **Loops** off for the exact speed, or choose a **Loop length** that fits it.
- **Problem:** a moving box stands still. **Why:** the preview's player is paused (or, in the viewport and exports, the material is baked at one moment). **Fix:** click **Play** under the preview.
