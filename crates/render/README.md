# render

**Layer 3 · Front end**

The viewport renderer: shaded meshes, wireframe overlay, selection highlights, grid, and GPU picking.

## Purpose

Draws the scene with wgpu into each viewport's own image: the calm gradient background, a fading grid, soft studio shading, anti-aliased wireframe, selection and hover highlights, the checker texture in UV mode. Also answers "what is under the cursor?" with an ID buffer and depth readback, which powers picking and orbiting around the point under the mouse. See DESIGN.md §4.6 and §5.

## Must never

- Change the scene: it only reads it
- Re-upload meshes that did not change
- Block the interface thread waiting on the GPU

## Depends on

`mesh` (the meshes to draw; `tools` only in tests, to aim the camera). Outside libraries: `wgpu` (GPU), `glam` (maths), `bytemuck` (GPU data).

## Status

The first 3D view, see [decision 0013](../../docs/decisions/0013-viewport-rendering.md):

- **`ViewportRenderer`** draws into a **`ViewportTarget`** (sRGB colour, 4× multisampling where supported, depth). `display_view` is the plain-RGBA view egui must be given.
- **Backdrop:** a studio sky over a slightly darker ground, drawn from each pixel's view direction (the inverse view-projection is in the frame uniforms), so the horizon follows the camera.
- **Grid** on the ground (X–Y plane, Z up), finite: it fades out around `FrameParams::focus` (the point the camera looks at), reaching farther as the camera zooms out (`GRID_MIN_REACH`, `GRID_REACH_PER_DISTANCE`). Drawn per pixel: a fine level, 1 m and 10 m lines (strengths from `grid_levels`) that fade with distance and density, out to 100 m. `FrameParams::fine_spacing` picks the fine level: `None` for only 1 m and 10 m lines, `Some(50.0)` for faint 50 cm lines (`HALF_METRE_STRENGTH`), `Some(10.0)` for 10 cm lines.
- **Axes:** X red and Y blue on the ground, Z green rising from the origin to 100 m (decision 0012), drawn in 1 m pieces (long lines that pass behind the camera are dropped by some drivers).
- **Shapes** (`Shape`): shaded faces with soft, wrapped studio light (gentle gradient across faces, slightly darker near the ground) and very faint white edges. `Shape::from_meshes` places any number of `mesh::Mesh`es in the world (`MeshInstance`: mesh, transform, selected), shading them from their hard and soft edges and drawing selected objects' edges in the colour it is given (the interface's accent); a mirrored placement keeps faces facing out. Faces are drawn from both sides (the back a little darker), and the grid is pushed just behind shapes lying on the ground. `Shape::test_cube` remains for the renderer's own tests and is what a new renderer starts with.
- **Object meshes** (`mesh_draw`, [decision 0035](../../docs/decisions/0035-heavy-meshes.md)): `MeshGeometry::new` turns a mesh into what the GPU draws, in its own space: `MeshVertex` (position and normal) per smooth group at each point, triangles and edges as index lists, and the average edge length. `MeshGeometry::moved` updates only the vertices around moved points and gives the ranges to upload; `forget_indices` lets go of the index lists once uploaded. `ViewportRenderer::set_mesh` uploads one under a key, `update_mesh` uploads ranges, `keep_meshes` forgets the rest, `mesh_count`. `set_draws` places them for drawing (`MeshDraw`: key, model matrix, edge colour, whether to draw edges, points with colour and size, and how faint a dense wireframe may fade), one uniform per object at a dynamic offset. Drawn with `mesh_face_vs`/`mesh_face_fs` (a mirrored placement shades right), `mesh_line_vs` (edges fade when they are only a few pixels apart on screen, keeping `faintest` of their colour) and `mesh_point_vs` (every point as a round dot, read once per dot). Picking draws them too. `MeshGeometry::shaded(mesh, hardness)` builds it shaded by a `mesh::Hardness` (by angle for Automatic hard edges, [decision 0064](../../docs/decisions/0064-automatic-hard-edges.md)); `hardness()` says which, and `can_follow` whether a move can be updated in place or needs it built afresh (always afresh with custom normals, [decision 0148](../../docs/decisions/0148-weighted-normals.md), which the normals follow through `mesh::corner_shading_with`). **Cavity** ([decision 0065](../../docs/decisions/0065-cavity.md)): the first stage (backdrop and surfaces; `PipelineKind::writes_facing`) also writes each pixel's facing across the screen into the target's facing image (`FACING_FORMAT`; `screen_right`/`screen_up` in the frame uniforms); with `set_show_cavity(true)` a second stage (`cavity_vs`/`cavity_fs`, reading it through its own bind group) lays light on ridges and shadow in creases; the grid, lines and overlay follow in a third stage. `set_show_faces(false)` leaves objects' faces out of the next frames (the Wireframe look, [decision 0063](../../docs/decisions/0063-wireframe.md)): their edges then show through; picking still uses the faces.
- **The numbered checker** ([decision 0107](../../docs/decisions/0107-numbered-checker.md)): `SurfaceDraw::NumberedChecker` (surface mode 3) reads the image given to `ViewportRenderer::set_grid_image` (an sRGB texture with every smaller copy, made by `halved`; repeating, smoothed), bound beside each object's uniforms (bindings 1 and 2 of group 1), with `textureSampleGrad`. Tested in `tests/gpu.rs`.
- **UVs, Checker and Stretch** ([decision 0102](../../docs/decisions/0102-checker-and-stretch.md)): each `MeshVertex` carries its UV, and `MeshGeometry` splits vertices where UVs do; `MeshGeometry::uv_density` is the mesh's texel density. `MeshDraw::surface` (`SurfaceDraw`: Plain, Checker { squares }, Stretch { density }) goes in the object uniforms (`surface`); the shader's `surface_color` draws the checker from the UV (tinted red across, green up) or the stretch per pixel from screen derivatives of the UV and world position (green, yellow at 1.5 times off, red at twice, purple with no room). Tested in `tests/gpu.rs`.
- **Material colours** ([decision 0074](../../docs/decisions/0074-material-slots.md)): each `MeshVertex` carries its polygons' material slot, and each `MeshDraw` a `Palette` of 64 packed sRGB colours (`palette(colors)`: slots past the last use the first; all 0 for the standard surface colour), sent in the object's uniforms; the shader turns them to linear light. Where materials meet on a soft edge, `MeshGeometry` makes one vertex per material with the same shading, and keeps them shaded together when points move.
- **Picking** (`pick`, decision 0017): `request_pick` draws the faces depth-only into a one-pixel image narrowed to the pixel under the mouse and copies the depth back; `poll_pick` collects the answer (a `PickResult` with the world point, or none for background) on a later frame without waiting. One question at a time; `pick_now` waits, for tests.
- **Overlay** (`Overlay`, `set_overlay`): the Points, Edges and Polygons modes' layer, drawn after the edges. `point` and `line` add screen-sized dots and thick lines (`Sprite`, one record each, made into two triangles by `sprite_vs`, which reads the image size from the frame uniforms), and `triangle` adds see-through tints. All are hidden behind faces by the depth test and write no depth, except `line_on_top` (`Overlay::on_top`, the `SpritesOnTop` pipeline with no depth test, drawn last): lines that show through every surface, for helpers that run through a shape ([decision 0158](../../docs/decisions/0158-bend-twist-taper.md)). Points are cut round in `sprite_fs` (corner distance, with a one-pixel soft rim); lines stay square-ended.
- **Flat views:** `FrameParams::flat_height` (set for an orthographic camera) gives a plain backdrop with no horizon, lines that don't fade with distance, and a grid reach based on the shown height. `FrameParams::grid_plane` sets the plane the grid lies in (`GROUND_PLANE` as standard), so front and side views see an upright grid face on.
- **Several views:** each view draws into its own `ViewportTarget`; `render` writes its settings and submits its own work, so several views can be drawn in one frame.
- **`read_pixels`** copies a finished image back, for tests.

Tests: the shader is validated without a GPU; `tests/gpu.rs` renders real frames (skipped without a GPU unless `REQUIRE_GPU` is set) and checks the sky above the horizon and the ground below it, the cube's shading order, the axes' colours, grid lines, the 50 cm and 10 cm grid levels, resizing, a flat front view (plain backdrop, upright grid), picking (the surface under a pixel, the background, a flat view, never waiting, broken requests), an empty shape (only the backdrop drawn, nothing picked), and object meshes (drawn and picked where placed, moved and forgotten; updated in place after a move; a mirrored placement shading like a plain one; every point shown as dots). An ignored heavy-mesh check times the geometry for a 3-million-polygon shape (`tools/heavy/heavy-check.sh`).

- **Outlines** ([decision 0135](../../docs/decisions/0135-live-booleans.md)): `MeshDraw::faces` off draws a mesh's edges only (a boolean's cutter): its faces are left out of the surface, cavity and pick passes. GPU test: `an_outline_only_mesh_shows_no_faces_and_is_not_picked`.
